The blue part connects the core datapath blocks together: the ALU, register file, PC logic, and control multiplexers. Instead of testing modules in isolation like the red stage, the blue block focuses on checking that these components interact correctly over time. Its job is to show that basic instruction-like operations work as a sequence: the PC updates every cycle, registers can be read and written, ALU operations produce correct results, and writeback feeds into the regfile on the next cycle. This verifies that the CPU’s dataflow and timing relationships are wired properly before the full instruction decode is added.

The verification testbench drives the blue datapath with simplified control signals rather than full instructions. Each cycle, the testbench selects register addresses, injects known operand values, chooses an ALU operation, and enables writeback. After a tick, the testbench checks that the hardware’s ALU result and register write match the expected C++ reference model. The PC path is also tested by toggling pc_en and alternate branch sources to ensure PC increments or redirects correctly. This multi-cycle testing catches wiring mistakes, ordering bugs, and timing misalignment between modules.

The Vbuddy version of the testbench provides a visual way to observe the datapath. The PC, ALU output, and writeback value can each be shown on a seven-segment digit. By slowing the simulation and driving a repeating pattern of operations, the Vbuddy display makes it easy to spot irregular behaviour: PC jumps, writeback failures, or ALU oscillations. The knob can be used to vary ALU control or register addresses in real time, turning the blue datapath into an interactive demonstration. Together, the deterministic tests, lightweight random testing, and Vbuddy visualisation confirm that the integrated datapath behaves consistently across sequential operations and is ready for full CPU-level instruction testing.
